Writing and presenting the assessment & plan

Make your reasoning visible: say what you think, why, what else it could be, and what you'll do about it.

Check each assessment: IDEA

I
Interpretive summary. One or two sentences that interpret the data, not repeat it: “troponin rising with ST depression,” not “trop 0.08.”
D
Differential with a commitment. Name the leading diagnosis, not just a list.
E
Explanation. The 2–3 findings that support it.
A
Alternatives. What else you considered and the finding that makes each less likely, plus the can't-miss diagnosis and how you're excluding it.

Pattern for each problem

#1 [Problem, with qualifiers: acuity, severity, likely cause] Most likely [dx] given [2–3 key findings]. Also considering [alt]; less likely because [finding against]. Must exclude [can't-miss] given [why]; [how]. - Diagnostic: what confirms it or rules out the others - Therapeutic: treatment for the leading dx - Monitoring / contingency: if ___, then ___

Habits that show reasoning

  • Interpret, don't repeat. “AKI, likely prerenal: BUN:Cr 28, FeNa 0.4%, poor intake” beats “Cr 2.1, BUN 59.”
  • Tie every plan item to the assessment. If you can't say which diagnosis a test confirms or excludes, question whether you need it.
  • Say what the alternatives lack. “Less likely because ___” shows reasoning; “r/o PE” alone doesn't.
  • Write contingencies. “If ___, then ___” shows you've thought ahead, and helps night float.
  • Order by acuity. Lead with what matters most today; group stable chronic problems at the end.

Presenting on rounds

  1. One-liner: age, key history with severity, why admitted, hospital day, and how they're doing.
  2. Overnight and today: events, symptoms, what the patient tells you. Keep it brief.
  3. Data with direction: vitals with trends, pertinent exam, labs as changes (“Cr 1.9, down from 2.3”), new imaging and micro.
  4. A&P by problem: lead with your assessment, then the plan. Commit: “I think this is ___ because ___. My plan is ___.”
  5. Close: disposition, barriers, and your questions for the team.

Pertinent only; “otherwise unremarkable” is fine. Know the why behind each plan item. If you don't know, say what you'd check.

Useful phrases

  • “Most consistent with ___ given ___.”
  • “Less likely given ___ / in the absence of ___.”
  • “Cannot exclude ___; will ___ to evaluate.”
  • “Improving / worsening / unchanged, as evidenced by ___.”
  • “If ___, will ___.”
  • “Precipitated by ___” / “in the setting of ___.”

Worked example: older adult with acute heart failure and pneumonia

A model, written for a made-up patient. Doses and thresholds are illustrative: check them against your references and your institution's protocols.

Summary: 82F with HFrEF (EF 30%), CKD 3b (baseline Cr 1.6), persistent AF on apixaban and T2DM, admitted with acute hypoxic respiratory failure from acute on chronic HFrEF exacerbation (warm and wet), most likely precipitated by RLL community-acquired pneumonia.

#1 Acute hypoxic respiratory failure, multifactorial

SpO2 88% on room air, now 92% on 3 L NC, explained by pulmonary edema (#2) and RLL pneumonia (#3). PE less likely with two clear explanations and therapeutic anticoagulation (confirm adherence). ACS less likely as a driver: troponin low and flat (0.04 → 0.04), EKG unchanged from prior.

  • Titrate O2 to SpO2 92–96%; incentive spirometry; out of bed to chair
  • Treat causes as below
  • If O2 need rises above 6 L or work of breathing increases: ABG, trial of BiPAP (helps with pulmonary edema), ICU evaluation

#2 Acute on chronic HFrEF (EF 30%), warm and wet

Supported by orthopnea, JVP ~12 cm, bibasilar crackles, 2+ leg edema, 4 kg above dry weight, BNP 2,400 (baseline ~600) and vascular congestion with small effusions on CXR. Most likely precipitated by pneumonia; ask about salt intake and missed doses. Ischemia less likely (see #1); AF is rate controlled, so not rate-driven.

  • Furosemide 80 mg IV BID (home 40 mg PO daily); goal net negative 1–2 L/day
  • Check response: spot urine Na 2 h after a dose or urine output over 6 h; if inadequate, double the dose
  • Strict I/O, daily standing weights, BMP twice daily while on IV diuretics; keep K ≥ 4, Mg ≥ 2
  • Continue metoprolol succinate, sacubitril/valsartan and empagliflozin while SBP > 100 and Cr stable; a small Cr rise with good decongestion is acceptable
  • 2 g sodium diet
  • If not decongesting by day 3: add a thiazide-type diuretic and involve cardiology

#3 RLL community-acquired pneumonia, non-severe

Supported by fever 38.4, productive cough, focal RLL consolidation with bronchial breath sounds and WBC 14.2. Edema alone less likely given fever and focal findings. Aspiration less likely: no dysphagia or witnessed event (ask). No risk factors for MRSA or Pseudomonas (no prior isolation, no IV antibiotics in the last 90 days), so no broad coverage. Not severe: no ICU criteria.

  • Ceftriaxone 1 g IV daily + azithromycin 500 mg daily × 3 days (QTc 450 ms; recheck if adding QT-prolonging drugs)
  • Blood cultures ×2 and sputum culture before antibiotics; influenza / COVID PCR
  • Switch to oral once afebrile and eating; total course 5 days if clinically stable
  • If not improving by 48–72 h: review cultures, image for effusion or empyema, broaden coverage

#4 AKI on CKD 3b (Cr 1.9, baseline 1.6)

Most likely venous congestion (cardiorenal) given JVD and edema; prerenal from poor intake less likely while volume overloaded. Expect improvement with decongestion.

  • Trend BMP with diuresis; renally dose medications; avoid NSAIDs and IV contrast
  • If Cr keeps rising with falling urine output: reassess volume status, urine studies, renal ultrasound

#5 Persistent AF, rate controlled (HR 80s–90s)

  • Continue metoprolol succinate; telemetry
  • Apixaban: meets 2 of 3 dose-reduction criteria (age ≥ 80, Cr ≥ 1.5), so 2.5 mg BID; confirm the home dose

#6 T2DM (A1c 7.2%)

  • Hold metformin during AKI; correctional insulin, goal glucose 140–180

Older adult, FEN / PPx / code / dispo

  • Delirium prevention: glasses and hearing aids, sleep hygiene, avoid benzodiazepines and anticholinergics; PT/OT; fall precautions
  • FEN: 2 g sodium, carbohydrate-consistent diet; replete K and Mg
  • VTE prophylaxis: on therapeutic apixaban
  • Code: full code, confirmed with patient; healthcare proxy daughter
  • Dispo: home with home health in 3–4 days, once off O2 (or qualified for home O2) and euvolemic on an oral diuretic
